# Cycle 159 — Process-tensor closure boundary

## Status

- Matching only one-time channels is sufficient to fix a multi-time experiment: **FALSIFIED**.
- Matching the complete quantum process tensor/comb and the same admissible intervention sequence fixes all corresponding multi-time outcome probabilities: **PROVED (standard operational composition; IMPORTED/KNOWN)**.
- Non-Markovian memory by itself supplies a beyond-QM PDT prediction: **FALSIFIED as a route**, because quantum process tensors/combs already include memory-bearing processes.
- PDT-native process outside the quantum-comb/process-tensor region, derived from identical microscopic controls: **OPEN**.
- Breakthrough candidate: **NO**.

## Theorem (conditional closure)

Fix a finite sequence of intervention times. Let Upsilon_R denote the quantum process tensor/comb induced by microscopic input I and declared resource window R. Let J(A_1),...,J(A_k) be the Choi operators of the same admissible instruments inserted at those times, and E_O the same terminal outcome effect. Multi-time probabilities are obtained by the standard multilinear/link-product contraction of Upsilon_R with these intervention operators and E_O.

Therefore, if PDT and QM use the identical Upsilon_R and identical interventions/effects, every multi-time probability is identical. A different narrative about memory, resource revelation, or environment records cannot change the probabilities while those operational objects remain fixed.

## Why pairwise channel matching is insufficient

A sequence of reduced two-time channels does not generally specify temporal correlations. Distinct environment memories can yield the same marginal/reduced maps at selected times while responding differently to intermediate interventions. Thus a same-input comparison for a non-Markovian experiment must fix or independently derive the complete multi-time process, not merely its one-time state trajectory or pairwise channels.

## Consequence for PDT-II target (3)

The candidate escape route

  same preparations + same marginal channels + non-Markovian PDT memory => beyond-QM probability gap

is **FALSIFIED as stated**. Quantum theory already permits non-Markovian process tensors/combs. A defensible PDT-vs-QM gap must instead derive, from identical laboratory controls, either:

1. a PDT process violating a necessary quantum-comb/process-tensor constraint; or
2. a stricter PDT-native inequality inside the quantum region whose parameters are independently fixed and whose QM comparator is not adjustable by hidden environment memory.

The first route is especially sharp: process Choi positivity and causal normalization constraints define a testable admissibility boundary. But PDT must derive a violation; none is assumed here.

## Prior-art boundary

This closure is not PDT novelty. Quantum combs/process tensors are established operational frameworks for arbitrary multi-time processes and non-Markovian memory. Relevant prior art includes Pollock et al., Phys. Rev. A 97, 012127 (2018), the process-tensor framework for non-Markovian quantum processes; White et al., PRX Quantum 3, 020344 (2022), non-Markovian process tomography; and work on quantum comb causal constraints. Accordingly this cycle is classified IMPORTED/KNOWN rather than breakthrough.

## Surviving obligation

Attack the quantum-process admissible region directly. Construct candidate PDT-native constraints from distinction/resource primitives, state exact hypotheses, then adversarially test Choi positivity, causal normalization, intervention closure, ancilla/reference extension, Markovian and non-Markovian cases, controlled environment records, and dimensions n=1..12 plus higher-dimensional random tests. Any proposed experimental inequality must survive ordinary quantum memory/environment explanations.
