# Cycle 160 — Causal-order escape boundary

## Status

- Fixed definite causal order is required by quantum mechanics itself: **FALSIFIED**.
- Indefinite/dynamical causal order alone supplies a beyond-QM PDT prediction: **FALSIFIED as a route**.
- A PDT causal-order principle could become distinctive only if it yields correlations outside the relevant quantum process-matrix/comb region, or a stricter PDT-native bound with independently fixed controls: **OPEN**.
- Breakthrough candidate: **NO**.

## Decisive boundary

Cycle 159 showed that ordinary non-Markovian memory is already captured by process tensors/combs. A tempting next escape is to claim that PDT can differ from QM by allowing the order of interventions itself to be indefinite or dynamically controlled.

That inference fails. The quantum process-matrix framework explicitly permits locally quantum experiments without assuming a predefined global causal order, and contains causally nonseparable processes. Quantum-control constructions such as the quantum switch also realize operational advantages associated with indefinite causal order while remaining within quantum theory.

Therefore the implication

    PDT indefinite causal order => beyond-QM prediction

is false. Indefinite order is not, by itself, a PDT-native discriminator.

## Surviving theorem obligation

For a proposed PDT multi-time object W_R, the comparison must first identify the applicable comparator set Q_R under the identical preparation, local instruments, controls, resource window and readout. A valid target-(3)/(5) result requires either

1. a rigorously PDT-derived W_R that violates a necessary quantum-process admissibility constraint while retaining normalized nonnegative probabilities for the declared PDT interventions; or
2. a PDT-native inequality B_PDT that is strictly stronger than the corresponding quantum bound B_Q for the same operational scenario, with all parameters independently fixed.

A violation of a *definite-order causal inequality* is not sufficient to establish beyond-QM physics because quantum process matrices can violate causal inequalities. Conversely, causal nonseparability alone is also insufficient: it is already a studied quantum-process property.

## Prior-art boundary

This is IMPORTED/KNOWN rather than PDT novelty. Oreshkov, Costa and Brukner (Nature Communications 3, 1092, 2012) introduced quantum correlations without predefined causal order and exhibited violation of a causal inequality. Oreshkov and Giarmatzi (New Journal of Physics 18, 093020, 2016) formalized causal and causally separable processes, including dynamical causal order and extensibility under ancillas. Later quantum-process work studies physical realizations, witnesses and bounds for indefinite causal order.

## Consequence for the search

Do not promote any PDT construction merely because it is non-Markovian, causally nonseparable, dynamically ordered, or violates a definite-order causal inequality. The next useful attack is the actual quantum-process admissible region: positivity/normalization under all declared local instruments, causal constraints appropriate to the scenario, arbitrary-reference extension where required, and quantitative quantum bounds. Any candidate must then be stress-tested across dimensions and adversarial instruments.

Classification: **PROVED / FALSIFIED / IMPORTED-KNOWN / OPEN**.
