# Cycle 224 — Functorial extension stability is not a composition selector

## Status

- **PROVED:** extension/functorial coherence is a consistency condition, not a uniqueness theorem for composites.
- **FALSIFIED:** the candidate claim that requiring admissibility to be stable under all ancillary extensions uniquely determines PDT composite admissibility.
- **IMPORTED/KNOWN:** non-uniqueness of GPT composites/tensor products and categorical composition structure are established neighboring ideas.
- **OPEN:** a PDT-native physical selector for the monoidal/composite structure; non-circular n=3; same-input PDT-vs-QM prediction.
- **BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE:** NO.

## Candidate claim attacked

> Once local admissibility is required to be functorial/extension-stable for every ancilla, the composite admissibility family is uniquely fixed.

This claim is false.

## Exact countermodel

For each finite system X let its microscopic states be a finite set. Local reversible operations are permutations. Compare two globally coherent theories sharing exactly the same one-system data.

### Theory L — local-generated composites

For a labelled bipartite system X×Y, admit the subgroup generated by maps

    (x,y) -> (sigma(x), tau(y))

(and wire swaps when the factor types permit them). Tensoring any admitted map with an identity on an arbitrary ancilla remains admitted. Composition and inverses remain admitted. Hence the family is extension-stable and monoidally coherent.

### Theory H — holistic composites

Use the same local systems and local permutations, but on each composite X×Y admit all permutations of X×Y. Again tensoring an admitted map with an identity ancilla is admitted; composition and inverses are admitted. Thus this family is also extension-stable and coherent.

For |X|=|Y|=2, H contains the controlled permutation

    C(x,y) = (x, y XOR x),

while L does not: C cannot be written as sigma×tau because the action on y depends on x. Therefore the two extension-stable families agree on every one-system reversible algebra but disagree on composite admissibility.

The obstruction embeds for every local cardinality n>=2 by restricting to a two-element sector. n=1 is degenerate and the two constructions coincide.

## Theorem

**Extension-stability non-uniqueness theorem.** Local admissibility data plus closure under composition, inverses, tensoring with identities, and ancillary extension do not uniquely determine composite admissibility. At least two inequivalent coherent completions can share all local data whenever nontrivial holistic reversible maps exist.

### Proof

The constructions L and H above both satisfy the stated closure properties. Their local restrictions coincide. For binary factors, C belongs to H. If C=sigma×tau, then its second output tau(y) would be independent of x; but C's second output y XOR x changes with x at fixed y, contradiction. Hence C is not in L. Therefore the hypotheses do not imply uniqueness. QED.

## Consequences for PDT-II

1. Functoriality/complete extension stability is necessary as a consistency test but cannot itself be the missing PDT-native composition law.
2. A PDT law must select *which* coherent monoidal completion nature realizes, using independently physical hypotheses rather than coherence alone.
3. Any n=3 result obtained only after choosing one coherent completion remains completion-dependent and therefore circular as a PDT derivation.
4. A same-input PDT/QM deviation cannot be manufactured by silently choosing different coherent composite completions; the microscopic coupling/admissibility specification must genuinely be identical or PDT must independently derive why QM's completion fails in the declared resource regime.

## Dimension stress logic

For n=1: no nonlocal controlled sector exists; expected degeneracy.
For each n=2,...,12: choose distinguished labels 0,1 and embed the binary controlled permutation on that 2×2 sector, identity elsewhere. The same factorization contradiction witnesses L != H.
For arbitrary n>=2 the same embedding proves the result exactly, so randomized higher-dimensional testing is unnecessary for the theorem itself.
