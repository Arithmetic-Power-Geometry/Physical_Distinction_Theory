# Cycle 261 — Local distinction data do not determine a composite: min/max tensor obstruction

## Targets attacked
1. PDT-native composition law.
2. Non-circular PDT-native n=3 derivation (indirectly: composition premise).
3. Same-input PDT-vs-QM prediction (consistency constraint).

## Candidate principle tested
A tempting PDT-native route is: **the joint system is fixed by preserving all local distinction probabilities and product tests**. In operational terms, start from local ordered state spaces `(V_A,C_A,u_A)` and `(V_B,C_B,u_B)`, require product states and product effects to be valid, positivity, normalization, and no signalling, and hope these distinction requirements uniquely fix the joint positive cone `C_AB`.

## Result — composition underdetermination theorem
Under those hypotheses the composite cone is constrained only by

`C_A ⊗_min C_B ⊆ C_AB ⊆ C_A ⊗_max C_B`,

where the minimal cone is generated by positive product tensors and the maximal cone is the dual of the minimal tensor product of the dual cones.

Therefore the local/product distinction data determine a unique composite **only if the admissible interval collapses**, i.e. `C_A ⊗_min C_B = C_A ⊗_max C_B`, or if an additional genuinely cross-system PDT axiom selects an intermediate cone.

### Proof
1. Validity of every positive product state implies every conical combination of positive product states is positive in the joint theory. Hence `C_min ⊆ C_AB`.
2. Validity of every positive product effect implies each `z ∈ C_AB` must satisfy `(f⊗g)(z) ≥ 0` for all positive local effects `f,g`. The set of tensors satisfying all such inequalities is exactly `C_max`; hence `C_AB ⊆ C_max`.
3. If `C_min != C_max`, at least two distinct joint cones satisfy the same local product-state/product-effect consistency requirements. Thus those requirements cannot uniquely determine composition. QED.

This is a structural obstruction, not a proposed new tensor product.

## Smallest decisive counterexample class
Whenever both local cones are non-simplicial/non-classical and their min/max tensor cones differ, choosing `C_AB=C_min` versus `C_AB=C_max` gives distinct composites while retaining the same local systems and all required product states/effects. The difference lies precisely in genuinely joint states/effects/correlations. Thus no scalar or pairwise PDT distinction rule that only reproduces local/product statistics can choose between them.

The exceptional collapse cases are important edge cases: classical simplicial cones can make min/max coincide against appropriate partners, so classical composition does not rescue a universal PDT uniqueness claim.

## Dimension stress
This obstruction is not a finite-n numerical accident. It is cone-theoretic and applies in every finite dimension in which the min/max interval is nontrivial. Hence testing n=1,...,12 cannot turn the insufficient local hypotheses into a unique composition law. n=1 is degenerate; classical/simplex cases must be separated from genuinely nonclassical cones; higher dimensions inherit the same interval obstruction whenever min != max.

## Same-input prediction consequence
This also sharpens target (3). If PDT uses exactly the same microscopic state, transformation, measurement/effect set, joint cone/composition, and resource-admissibility window as QM, then the operational probabilities are the same by construction. A claim `P_PDT(O|I,R) != P_QM(O|I,R)` therefore requires PDT to specify at least one altered operational primitive (joint cone/state, allowed effect, transformation/dynamics, probability rule, or resource-dependent admissibility). A resource label alone is not a probability-changing mechanism.

## Prior-art audit
The min/max tensor interval is standard ordered-vector-space/GPT structure, not PDT novelty. Modern cone-tensor literature explicitly uses `C_min ⊆ C_AB ⊆ C_max`; work on entangleability of cones establishes strong conditions for equality/inequality. Distinguishability norms under restricted measurement classes and data hiding are also established quantum/GPT topics. Therefore the obstruction theorem is recorded as an audit constraint, **not** promoted as a PDT breakthrough.

## What survives for PDT-II
A viable PDT-native composition theorem must add a cross-system primitive that distinguishes admissible points inside the min/max interval without simply importing local tomography, quantum tensor products, self-duality, purification, entanglement, or another known reconstruction axiom under new terminology. Candidate future attacks should formulate that primitive operationally and then test associativity, symmetry, positivity, reversible-group compatibility, quotient/resource refinement, and min/max/Jordan countermodels.
