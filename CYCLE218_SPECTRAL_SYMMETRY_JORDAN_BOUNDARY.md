# Cycle 218 — Spectrality + Strong Symmetry Is a Jordan Boundary, Not a PDT Composition Law

## Target attacked
PDT-II priorities (1) PDT-native composition and (2) non-circular n=3 derivation, with the Cycle-217 warning that imported reconstruction axioms cannot simultaneously serve as PDT novelty.

## Candidate principle
Try to select the PDT operational state space by imposing:

1. **Spectrality:** every normalized state is a convex combination of a finite frame of perfectly distinguishable pure states.
2. **Strong symmetry:** for every frame size k, the reversible group acts transitively on ordered k-frames.

Question: do these conditions uniquely select the complex-quantum state space/composite, and hence provide a PDT-native route to n=3?

## Result
**FALSIFIED as a uniqueness/PDT-native selection claim.**

A known theorem of Barnum and Hilgert shows that finite-dimensional strongly symmetric spectral compact convex state spaces are precisely simplices and normalized state spaces of finite-dimensional simple Euclidean Jordan algebras (EJAs). Thus the candidate package lands in an already-classified Jordan family rather than selecting complex quantum theory.

The family contains inequivalent alternatives, including real, complex and quaternionic matrix EJAs, spin factors, and the exceptional Albert algebra. Consequently spectrality + strong symmetry cannot by themselves select a unique PDT composite or a unique dimension-3 structure.

## Exact dimension obstruction
For an n-level matrix system, the real vector-space dimensions of Hermitian state spaces over the standard division algebras differ while the Jordan rank n can be the same:

- real symmetric matrices: d_R(n) = n(n+1)/2;
- complex Hermitian matrices: d_C(n) = n^2;
- quaternionic Hermitian matrices: d_H(n) = n(2n-1).

For n=3 this gives 6, 9, and 15 respectively. Hence the same spectral rank n=3 does not determine the complex-quantum 9-dimensional Hermitian representation, still less a PDT-specific three-dimensional physical law.

For n=1..12 these three dimension sequences coincide only at n=1; for every n>=2 they are pairwise distinct. This is algebraic, not merely numerical.

## Composition boundary
Adding local tomography after obtaining Jordan systems is additional structure, not a consequence of spectrality + strong symmetry. Barnum-Wilce show that, under further assumptions on systems/composites and with a qubit, Jordan structure plus local tomography can single out ordinary finite-dimensional complex quantum mechanics (with superselection qualifications). That route therefore imports a quantum-reconstruction package rather than deriving a PDT-native composition law.

## Same-input prediction consequence
If PDT adopts sufficient extra assumptions to collapse the Jordan alternatives to standard complex QM on a declared input/resource domain, Cycle 217 applies: without an additional PDT prediction-changing law, the same operational state/effect/transformation structure gives the same P(O|I,R). Therefore this route cannot simultaneously derive QM and generate a PDT-vs-QM deviation.

## Prior-art boundary
- Barnum & Hilgert, *Strongly symmetric spectral convex bodies are Jordan algebra state spaces*, arXiv:1904.03753.
- Koecher–Vinberg: homogeneous self-dual cones correspond to finite-dimensional Euclidean/formally-real Jordan algebra structure.
- Barnum & Wilce, *Local tomography and the Jordan structure of quantum theory*, Foundations of Physics 44 (2014), arXiv:1202.4513.

These are established reconstruction/GPT results. No PDT novelty is claimed for the Jordan classification.

## Smallest decisive witness
n=2 already separates the matrix families:

d_R(2)=3, d_C(2)=4, d_H(2)=6.

Thus the uniqueness failure occurs before n=3.

## Surviving requirement
A viable PDT-II route must introduce a PDT-native physical principle that selects an admissible joint-operation/observation structure **before** the desired complex-quantum/Jordan answer is assumed. If it instead adopts a known reconstruction package sufficient to recover complex QM, it cannot claim that package as PDT novelty or use it alone to obtain a same-input deviation.
