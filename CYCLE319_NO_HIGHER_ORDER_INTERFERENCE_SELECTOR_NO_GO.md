# PDT-II Cycle 319 — No-higher-order-interference does not select n=3 or a unique composition

## Target attacked
Highest-priority unresolved targets: PDT-native composition law and non-circular PDT-native n=3 derivation.

## Candidate principle
Strengthen the surviving operational requirements by requiring **no genuine third- or higher-order interference** (in the Sorkin/Barnum–Müller–Ududec operational sense), together with classical decomposability and strong symmetry. Hope: perhaps this physically meaningful distinction/refinement principle uniquely selects a three-dimensional PDT structure or standard complex-quantum composition.

## Verdict
**FALSIFIED as an n=3/unique-theory selector; IMPORTED/KNOWN mechanism.**

Barnum, Müller and Ududec prove that classical decomposability + strong symmetry + no higher-order interference restrict finite-dimensional systems to classical systems or irreducible formally real Jordan systems, including real, complex and quaternionic quantum theories, spin factors of arbitrary dimension, and the exceptional 3x3 octonionic case. Thus absence of third-order interference does not select a unique three-dimensional system and does not by itself select complex quantum theory.

Adding their further single-system postulate, observability of energy, selects complex quantum theory. That is a known reconstruction result and therefore cannot be relabelled as a PDT-native derivation.

References:
- Barnum, Müller, Ududec, Higher-order interference and single-system postulates characterizing quantum theory, New J. Phys. 16 (2014) 123029, arXiv:1403.4147.
- Barnum, Lee, Scandolo, Selby, Ruling out Higher-Order Interference from Purity Principles, Entropy 19 (2017) 253, arXiv:1704.05106.

## Dimension stress consequence
The counterfamily is not confined to one exceptional dimension: the known solution class contains spin-factor state spaces of arbitrary finite dimension and multiple real/complex/quaternionic matrix families. Therefore tests at n=1,...,12 cannot turn the candidate into an n=3 selector; members satisfying the candidate occur across dimensions. This is an analytic family-level counterexample, stronger than finite numerical sampling.

## Same-input prediction consequence

Standard complex quantum theory itself satisfies the no-higher-order-interference condition, while other allowed Jordan models remain. Choosing one non-quantum member would import an additional selector rather than derive it from PDT.
