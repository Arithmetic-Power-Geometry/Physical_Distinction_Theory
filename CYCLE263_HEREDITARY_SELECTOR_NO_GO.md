# Cycle 263 — Hereditary dimension-selector no-go

## Targets attacked
1. PDT-native composition law.
2. Non-circular PDT-native n=3 derivation.
3. Same-input PDT-vs-QM prediction.
5. Experimentally distinctive inequalities.

## Candidate class
Many attempted PDT-II selectors are properties witnessed entirely inside a finite operational sector: a collection of states/effects/transformations supported on a proper face/subspace, with the resource window restricted to that sector. Examples already tested in this branch include CHSH/Tsirelson witnesses, no-broadcasting witnesses, purification examples, interference-order tests, continuous reversibility examples, and restricted distinguishability norms.

Define a predicate A on a family of operational theories T_n to be **embedding-hereditary upward** when, whenever an admissible resource-preserving embedding E_{m->n}:T_m -> T_n exists, every witness establishing A in T_m remains a witness in T_n.

## Theorem — hereditary selector obstruction
Let {T_n}_{n>=1} be a nested family with resource-preserving embeddings E_{m->n} for m<=n. Let A be an embedding-hereditary upward predicate. If A(T_k) holds, then A(T_n) holds for every n>=k. Therefore A cannot uniquely select any finite dimension n*>k. In particular, if A(T_2) holds, A cannot uniquely select n=3.

### Proof
Fix n>=k. By hypothesis E_{k->n} is an admissible resource-preserving embedding. Since A(T_k) holds and A is embedding-hereditary upward, the image under E_{k->n} is a valid witness for A(T_n). Hence A(T_n) holds. Thus the truth set contains the entire upper tail {k,k+1,...}; it cannot equal {n*} for any finite n*>k. QED.

## Stronger finite-support corollary
Suppose an operational inequality or principle is evaluated only from probabilities generated by states, effects and dynamics contained in a k-dimensional invariant sector, and the declared resource window is preserved by zero-padding/isometric embedding. Then its value is exactly unchanged under embedding into every n>=k. Such a witness can certify at most a lower bound on dimension; it cannot certify that the ambient dimension is exactly 3.

For complex QM this follows directly from V:C^k -> C^n: rho -> V rho V^†, E -> V E V^† (with complementary outcomes completed on the orthogonal sector), and Phi -> V Phi(V^† · V)V^† on the occupied sector. Born probabilities Tr(E rho) are invariant. Tensor-product experiments supported on embedded local sectors inherit the same equality.

## n=1..12 stress
For k=2 the theorem predicts the truth pattern FALSE/degenerate at n=1 and TRUE for every n=2..12 for any selector whose witness is fully qubit-supported and embedding-hereditary. The accompanying exact harness checks probability preservation for rational pure/mixed diagonal states and rational POVM effects under zero-padding for every n=2..12; no floating-point tolerance is needed.

The theorem also covers arbitrarily high finite n analytically, so randomized higher-dimensional testing is not evidence beyond the proof. Degenerate n=1 must be treated separately.

## Consequence for PDT-II search
This eliminates an entire strategy class rather than one named axiom. A non-circular n=3 derivation must contain at least one **non-hereditary, ambient-sensitive** ingredient: a principle whose truth/value changes when a valid 3-dimensional model is embedded into dimension 4 or higher, or a maximality/minimality condition that independently forbids the extra sector. Merely exhibiting a qutrit witness is insufficient.

Likewise, a same-input experimental inequality that is supported wholly in a common k-dimensional sector cannot distinguish PDT from QM if both theories assign the same states, effects, dynamics and admissible resource window on that sector. A deviation requires an operational primitive that is not preserved by the common embedding.

## Prior-art boundary
The mathematical implication above is elementary and is **not claimed as novel**. Hardy's reconstruction explicitly uses a subspace principle in which an M-dimensional constrained system behaves as an M-dimensional system. Dimension-witness literature (Brunner et al. 2008; Gallego et al. 2010) is the relevant operational boundary: dimension witnesses provide lower bounds on required dimension, showing why genuinely dimension-sensitive statistics are needed. The value here is as a PDT-II pruning theorem unifying many failed selector routes, not as a new theorem of quantum foundations.
