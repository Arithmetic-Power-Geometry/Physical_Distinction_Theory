# Cycle 260 — Entangling-composite route can select n=3, but it is prior art rather than PDT-native

## Targets attacked
1. PDT-native composition law.
2. Non-circular PDT-native derivation of n=3.

## Candidate principle
Restrict the elementary two-level state space to a Euclidean n-ball, require continuous reversible local dynamics and local tomography for bipartite systems, and additionally require the composite to contain entangled states / a nontrivial reversible interaction.

## Exact hypotheses and scope
This cycle does **not** claim that PDT derives these hypotheses. It asks whether the tempting route “ball + reversible symmetry + locally tomographic composite + entanglement selects n=3” is available as a new PDT-II theorem.

## Prior-art result
Masanes, Müller, Pérez-García & Augusiak, *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 55, 122203 (2014), “Entanglement and the three-dimensionality of the Bloch ball”, classify bipartite state spaces built from d-dimensional Euclidean-ball local systems under continuous/reversible dynamics and local tomography and show that, except for the quantum two-qubit case, the classified composites contain no entangled states. Thus the n=3 selection mechanism already exists in the GPT reconstruction literature.

A closely related reconstruction boundary is de la Torre, Masanes, Short & Müller, *Physical Review Letters* 109, 090403 (2012): for identical local qubits, admitting at least one continuous reversible interaction recovers the global quantum structure. Hardy’s reconstruction programme and later GPT work likewise use composite/local-tomographic structure as substantive axioms rather than deriving it from a scalar distinction measure.

## Dimension stress interpretation
For n=1,...,12, the candidate is not a dimension-blind axiom of the kind falsified in Cycles 250–259: under the cited classification, n=3 is exceptional once the full composite assumptions and entanglement requirement are imposed. Therefore it would be incorrect to manufacture an n=2 embedding counterexample while retaining all of these hypotheses. The correct result is a **prior-art boundary**, not a falsification of the published selector.

The dimension table is therefore logically:
- n=3: compatible with the ordinary two-qubit entangling composite.
- n in {1,2,4,...,12}: excluded by the cited classified Euclidean-ball + composite hypothesis package if entanglement is required.
- higher finite n: excluded by the same classification, subject to its exact assumptions.

No numerical experiment is promoted as evidence here because the published theorem is analytic and because reproducing a theorem numerically would not create PDT novelty.

## PDT consequence
This route cannot be promoted as a PDT-native n=3 breakthrough unless PDT independently derives at least one genuinely new premise that forces the relevant composite assumptions from PDT primitives. In particular, simply renaming local tomography, continuous reversibility, entanglement, or nontrivial reversible interaction in “distinction” language is not enough.

Likewise, the published selector does not automatically furnish a PDT-native composition law: it classifies composites under already substantive local/composite assumptions. PDT-II still owes a native cross-system operation/cone rule or a theorem deriving those assumptions from PDT primitives.

## Adversarial novelty check
Attempted novelty claim: “PDT derives n=3 because only a 3-ball supports entanglement under natural reversible composition.”

Result: **REJECTED AS REDISCOVERY**. The mathematical selection mechanism is established prior art. A PDT theorem must have different hypotheses or a genuinely new derivation from independently defined PDT primitives.
