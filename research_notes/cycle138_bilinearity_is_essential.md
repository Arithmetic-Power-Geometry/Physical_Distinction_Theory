# Cycle 138 — Bilinearity is essential for the full-isotropy n=3 selector

## Target
Attack the remaining bridge behind Cycle 137: determine whether weaker PDT-natural assumptions such as self-nullness/alternation plus full proper-rotation covariance already force the n=3 selector.

## Decisive counterfamily
For every real dimension n>=1 define

\[
B_n(x,y)=\|y\|^2x-\|x\|^2y.
\]

This map is nonzero, continuous, polynomial, self-null, alternating, and fully O(n)-equivariant (hence SO(n)-equivariant):

\[
B_n(Rx,Ry)=R B_n(x,y)
\]

for every orthogonal R, because Euclidean norms are rotation invariant. Also

\[
B_n(y,x)=-B_n(x,y),\qquad B_n(x,x)=0.
\]

But it is not bilinear. The smallest exact witness is already n=1 with x=1,y=2:

\[
B(1,2)=2,\qquad B(2,2)=0\ne 4=2B(1,2).
\]

Therefore the implication

\[
\text{nonzero} + \text{alternating/self-null} + SO(n)\text{-covariant} \Longrightarrow n=3
\]

is false. The bilinearity/linear-response hypothesis in Cycle 137 is logically essential, not cosmetic.

## Proof of covariance and alternation
For orthogonal R,

\[
\|Rx\|=\|x\|,\qquad \|Ry\|=\|y\|,
\]

so

\[
B(Rx,Ry)=\|y\|^2Rx-\|x\|^2Ry=R B(x,y).
\]

Swapping x and y immediately changes the sign. Thus the counterexample is exact in every dimension; the numerical audit is only a regression/stress check.

## Prior-art boundary
This construction is not claimed as novel mathematics. Its ingredients are standard orthogonal-invariant/equivariant structure: scalar coefficients may be formed from inner products/norms and multiplied into the input vectors. Classical invariant theory for O(n) treats scalar invariants as functions generated by pairwise inner products. The PDT contribution, if any, would have to be a new operational reason that excludes such nonlinear equivariant responses.

## Strengthened surviving obligation
Cycle 137 remains a valid conditional theorem, but Cycle 138 shows exactly what PDT must still supply:

\[
\boxed{\text{PDT primitives} \Longrightarrow \text{bilinear (or equivalent first-order linear-response) distinction interaction}.}
\]

A merely symmetric, isotropic, antisymmetric or self-null interaction is insufficient. A defensible PDT-native derivation must identify an operational composition/superposition principle whose local response forces separate linearity in each input. That principle must itself be dimension-independent and must not encode the desired n=3 conclusion.
