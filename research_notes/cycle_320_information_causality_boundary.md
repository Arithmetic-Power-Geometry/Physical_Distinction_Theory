# PDT-II Cycle 320 — Information-causality selector boundary

## Target
Attack the strongest unresolved PDT-II targets: a PDT-native composition selector, non-circular n=3 selection, and a same-input quantitative PDT/QM deviation.

## Candidate principle
Use information causality (IC), possibly together with no-signalling and the previously tested coherence/tomography requirements, as the missing selector of the physically admissible correlation/composite structure.

## Prior-art audit (22 Sep 2026)
This route is not PDT-native. Information causality is an established quantum-foundations principle. The established literature shows that ordinary IC reproduces important quantum boundaries such as the CHSH/Tsirelson bound but historically did not characterize the full quantum set in general scenarios. Almost-quantum correlations were introduced specifically as a post-quantum set satisfying many proposed physical principles. A new Sep-2026 result by Gachechiladze and Miklin reports that a generalized IC formulation with correlated inputs and a new communication protocol derives the exact Tsirelson–Landau–Masanes criterion in the simplest bipartite two-binary-measurement scenario. That result is important but scenario-specific and imported prior art, not a PDT derivation.

## Prove-or-falsify result
### Claim A
"Information causality by itself is a new PDT-native composition law."

**FALSIFIED / IMPORTED-KNOWN.** IC predates PDT and cannot be promoted as a PDT-native law merely by relabelling it in distinction language.

### Claim B
"The known IC principle uniquely fixes the complete composite/correlation structure required by PDT-II."

**FALSIFIED as a general inference from established IC.** Historical IC constraints capture important quantum boundaries but do not supply a known all-scenario uniqueness theorem. The Sep-2026 generalized-IC result closes the simplest 2x2 binary Bell-correlation boundary, not arbitrary state spaces, tensor products, dynamics, dimensions, thermodynamic resources, or the full PDT composite structure.

### Claim C
"IC yields a non-circular physical n=3 selector."

**FALSIFIED as an inference.** Neither the original IC principle nor the new 2x2 generalized-IC characterization singles out three spatial dimensions or a three-outcome ontology. The successful theorem is explicitly formulated in a bipartite two-input/two-output Bell scenario, so importing it cannot constitute a derivation of n=3.

### Claim D
"IC alone supplies a same-input parameter-free P_PDT != P_QM prediction."

**FALSIFIED as an inference.** Quantum correlations obey IC, and the new generalized-IC result recovers the quantum boundary in the simplest Bell scenario. Therefore no PDT/QM probability difference follows unless PDT first supplies an additional independently derived rule selecting correlations outside or inside QM differently.

## Surviving boundary
A viable PDT-native selector must do more than impose an already-known information principle. It must derive, from PDT primitives, a rule that fixes correlated composite directions (or state/effect cones), survives the earlier crossnorm/GPT counterexamples, and then produces either (i) a rigorous uniqueness theorem not inherited from known reconstructions, or (ii) an explicit same-input operational probability differing from QM within a declared resource window.

## Dimension/status stress
The failure here is structural rather than a finite-n numerical accident: the candidate principle contains no mechanism selecting n=3, so testing n=1..12 cannot create such a selector. No numerical simulation is claimed in this cycle.
