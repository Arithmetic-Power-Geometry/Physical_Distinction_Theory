# Cycle 124 — SO(n)-covariant same-sector bilinear selector

## Question attacked
Can the remaining PDT composition bottleneck be connected to a non-circular dimension selector without importing a normed cross product, Jacobi identity, or octonionic exclusion by hand?

## Exact conditional theorem
Let V=R^n with its Euclidean inner product, n>=2. Assume there is a **nonzero bilinear same-sector operation**

B: V x V -> V

that is equivariant under every orientation-preserving rotation:

B(Rx,Ry)=R B(x,y) for every R in SO(n).

Then n=3.

Equivalently,

Hom_SO(n)(V tensor V,V) != {0}  iff  n=3     (n>=2).

For n=3 this one-dimensional intertwiner space is generated by the ordinary cross product / Levi-Civita tensor.

### Proof boundary
Lower the output index with the invariant Euclidean metric:

T(x,y,z)=<B(x,y),z>.

T is an SO(n)-invariant rank-3 tensor. Standard orthogonal invariant theory says SO(n)-invariant tensors are generated by contractions of the metric delta (rank 2) and the orientation tensor epsilon (rank n). A rank-3 invariant therefore occurs for n>=2 only at n=3, where epsilon_ijk supplies it. For even n an elementary sub-proof is immediate because -I is in SO(n): bilinearity yields B(-x,-y)=B(x,y), whereas equivariance yields B(-x,-y)=-B(x,y), forcing B=0. The invariant-tensor argument eliminates odd n>=5.

This is standard representation/invariant theory and is **not claimed as PDT novelty**. Relevant prior-art boundary: Hermann Weyl's orthogonal invariant theory and modern treatments of tensor invariants, e.g. Draisma & Regts, *Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics* 38 (2013), 393–405, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-012-0408-7 .

## Decisive reflection falsification
If PDT's word "isotropy" is interpreted as **full O(n) covariance**, then no nonzero bilinear same-sector operation survives in any dimension.

Because -I is in O(n) for all n,

B(-x,-y)=B(x,y)  [bilinearity]

but

B(-x,-y)=-B(x,y) [O(n)-equivariance],

hence B=0.

In n=3 the usual cross product therefore transforms as an axial vector, not an ordinary polar vector, under reflections. Exact witness:

R=diag(-1,1,1), x=e2, y=e3.

Then

(Rx) x (Ry)=e1,
R(x x y)=-e1,

with residual norm 2.

Thus any PDT route using the 3D cross product must distinguish orientation-preserving SO(3) covariance from full reflection covariance. Treating them as identical silently kills the desired operation.

## Stress audit
The exact intertwiner dimension was tabulated for n=1..12 and n=16,24,32,48,64,96,128:

- SO(n): one-dimensional at n=1 (trivial group) and n=3; zero otherwise.
- O(n): zero in every tested dimension, and the -I proof is dimension-independent.

A deterministic numerical regression with seed 124 tested 1000 random SO(3) rotations and random vector pairs. Standard cross-product covariance had 0 violations at tolerance 1e-10; maximum floating residual was 4.070144838902081e-15. This is regression evidence only; the theorem is exact.

## Classification
- **PROVED:** the conditional representation-theoretic selector.
- **CONDITIONAL:** depends on a nonzero bilinear same-sector operation and SO(n) covariance.
- **IMPORTED/KNOWN:** invariant-theory machinery and the SO(3) cross product are standard mathematics.
- **NUMERICALLY SUPPORTED:** 1000 SO(3) covariance regression trials, 0 violations at 1e-10.
- **FALSIFIED:** replacing SO(n) by full O(n) kills every nonzero bilinear operation.
- **OPEN:** PDT has not yet derived same-sector bilinearity or the physical reason that orientation reversal should act axially rather than as ordinary O(n) covariance.

## Why this advances the bottleneck
Earlier PDT routes needed normed vector cross products plus Jacobi coherence, which imported substantial structure. This theorem shows that, *if* PDT can independently derive merely a nonzero bilinear same-sector composition and orientation-preserving rotational covariance, n=3 follows immediately without a norm condition, Jacobi, alternativity, or a separate n=7 exclusion.

That is a sharper conditional bridge, but it is **not a breakthrough candidate** until the two hypotheses are derived from PDT primitives rather than stipulated.
