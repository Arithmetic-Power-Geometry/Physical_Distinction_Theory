# Cycle 279 — Global revelation beyond product resources is not a PDT-specific signature

Status: **DECISIVE FALSIFICATION of a candidate PDT-specific experimental route; IMPORTED/KNOWN quantum/GPT phenomenon**

## Question attacked
After Cycle 278 isolated the joint admissible-effect generator as the missing composition object, could PDT obtain an experimentally distinctive inequality simply from the existence of joint/global tests that reveal distinctions invisible or strongly suppressed under product/local tests?

## Candidate claim
A tempting candidate is:

> If a bipartite pair has small or zero distinction under a declared product/local resource family but larger distinction after access to a global joint resource, then the excess is a PDT-specific revelation effect.

This candidate is false as a PDT-specific claim.

## Operational formulation
For a measurement family M define the restricted binary distinction norm/bias schematically by

`Delta_M(rho,sigma) = sup_{E in M} |Tr[E(rho-sigma)]|`

(up to conventional factors depending on the binary-effect/POVM normalization).

For nested resource families `M_local subseteq M_global`, monotonicity is immediate:

`Delta_local <= Delta_global`.

The gap

`G = Delta_global - Delta_local >= 0`

or a corresponding ratio quantifies additional distinction unlocked by the enlarged measurement family.

## Decisive prior-art collision
This phenomenon is already standard quantum information. Quantum data hiding provides bipartite state pairs that are highly/perfectly distinguishable by unrestricted global measurements while being poorly distinguishable under LOCC or other restricted measurement classes. Matthews, Wehner and Winter (Commun. Math. Phys. 291, 813–843, 2009; arXiv:0810.2327) explicitly study distinguishability norms generated by restricted measurement families and their application to quantum data hiding. Lami, Palazuelos and Winter (2017, arXiv:1703.03392) analyze data-hiding ratios in quantum mechanics and GPTs and connect them to tensor norms. Janotta and Lal (Phys. Rev. A 87, 052131, 2013) also show that restrictions on effect spaces are genuine additional GPT structure rather than something fixed automatically by the state space.

Therefore `Delta_global > Delta_local`, even dramatically so, is not a same-input PDT-vs-QM deviation. Standard microscopic quantum mechanics supplied with the same local/global measurement restrictions predicts such gaps.

## Smallest structural witness
No high dimension is needed to falsify the logical inference. Let a joint difference space contain a direction `x` annihilated by every functional in a restricted product/local span `W_L`, while some allowed global functional `g` satisfies `g(x) != 0`. Then

`Delta_L(x)=0`, while `Delta_G(x)>0`

for `W_G = span(W_L,g)`.

This is exactly the linear-algebraic revelation mechanism isolated after Cycle 278. Physical quantum data-hiding constructions supply nontrivial realizations under operationally meaningful restricted families.

## Dimension stress logic
The logical counterexample to PDT-specificity needs only one valid standard-QM realization. Once a realization exists in a finite bipartite sector, it can be embedded into larger Hilbert spaces by adjoining unused orthogonal levels while preserving the relevant state pair and allowed measurement protocol on the active sector. Thus observing a local/global revelation gap cannot select elementary `n=3` either. The exact quotient/rank bookkeeping from Cycle 278 remains valid only under its declared factorized-span hypothesis; global effects can add resolved joint directions.

## What survives
A PDT-specific experimental inequality must not merely compare a restricted measurement class with a richer one. It must predict a quantitative bound or outcome that standard microscopic QM, given **the identical state preparation, measurement family, resource budget, and side information**, does not predict.

A potentially meaningful PDT target therefore has to constrain how a physical resource window `R` generates the admissible joint family `M_R` in a way that is independently measurable and stricter/different from standard quantum resource accounting. Only then can a bound involving `Delta_{M_R}` become PDT-specific.

## Prior-art boundary
- Restricted-measurement distinguishability norms: **IMPORTED/KNOWN**.
- Quantum data hiding/local-vs-global distinguishability gaps: **IMPORTED/KNOWN**.
- GPT freedom/restriction of effect spaces: **IMPORTED/KNOWN**.
- Generic nesting monotonicity `Delta_local <= Delta_global`: **PROVED but elementary/known**.
- `Delta_global > Delta_local` as a uniquely PDT experimental signature: **FALSIFIED**.
- Revelation gap as a route to `n=3`: **FALSIFIED**.
- PDT-native law deriving `M_R` from physical resource variables with a same-input deviation from QM: **OPEN**.

## Consequence for the breakthrough program
Do not promote resource-revelation or data-hiding-like gaps themselves as PDT-II novelty. The strongest remaining composition/prediction target is now narrower: derive a non-circular map

`R -> M_R`

(or a rigorously weaker operational object) from PDT-native physical constraints, and then test whether that map implies a same-input quantitative prediction unavailable from standard QM with the same declared resources.
