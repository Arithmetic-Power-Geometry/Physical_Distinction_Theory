# Cycle 140 — Raywise second-order response no-go

## Target
Test whether the Cycle-139 joint-Hessian assumption can be weakened to the physically tempting statement that every positive ray through the null state has exact quadratic response.

## Counterfamily
For every real dimension n>=1 define

B(x,y) = ||y|| x - ||x|| y.

It is continuous, nonzero, alternating, sector-null, and O(n)-equivariant. For every t>=0,

B(tx,ty) = t^2 B(x,y).

Thus each positive ray has an exact second-order coefficient at the null state:

C(x,y) = lim_{t downarrow 0} 2 B(tx,ty)/t^2 = 2 B(x,y).

Nevertheless C is not generated by a joint bilinear Hessian. The smallest witness is n=1 with u=(1,0) and v=(0,-1). Here B(u)=B(v)=0, B(u+v)=2, and B(u-v)=0, so the quadratic parallelogram identity fails by 2 (squared gap 4). Therefore the ray coefficients do not polarize to a symmetric bilinear second derivative.

## Result
**FALSIFIED:** continuity + alternation + sector-nullness + full rotational covariance + exact positive-ray degree-two scaling does not imply bilinearity and does not select n=3.

**PROVED:** the displayed counterfamily satisfies those hypotheses algebraically in every n.

**NUMERICALLY SUPPORTED:** the regression audit covers n=1..12,16,24,32,48,64,96,128 with 830 seeded cases and zero covariance, homogeneity, antisymmetry, or sector-null failures. Maximum covariance residual was 1.054379253234475e-15 and maximum positive-homogeneity residual was 9.777686967753793e-16.

**IMPORTED/KNOWN boundary:** the distinction between directional/Gateaux-type information and stronger Frechet differentiability is standard analysis. PDT novelty is not claimed for that mathematical fact. This cycle uses it only to identify the precise missing PDT-native bridge.

**OPEN:** PDT must derive a genuinely joint two-direction second-order law: e.g. second Frechet differentiability at the null state, a valid polarization/parallelogram law for the response coefficient, or an operational superposition principle strong enough to imply one of these. Merely measuring a quadratic coefficient independently along every positive ray is insufficient.

**BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE:** NO.
