# Cycle 117 — Finite revelation does not force same-sector closure

## Status

**PROVED + FALSIFIED + IMPORTED/KNOWN + NUMERICALLY SUPPORTED + OPEN**

## Question attacked

Cycle 116 left a possible escape route: perhaps PDT can force `V x V -> V` by demanding that every auxiliary degree of freedom generated by composition must become observable after a finite resource refinement. Cycle 117 tests that proposal directly.

## Exact countermodel

Let `V=M=R^n` and `A=V+M`. Define coordinatewise

`(a,m)*(b,r) = (a*b, a*r + m*b + lambda*a*b)`

with fixed nonzero `lambda`. As proved in Cycle 116 this product is associative.

Use two declared resource windows:

- coarse window `R0`: `pi0(a,m)=a`;
- refined finite window `R1`: `pi1(a,m)=(a,m)`.

`pi1` is injective, so every auxiliary component is revealed exactly after one finite refinement. Nevertheless two visible-sector states obey

`(a,0)*(b,0)=(a*b,lambda*a*b)`,

which generically leaves the visible sector.

Thus

`finite exact revealability + exact coarse quotient composition + associativity`

**does not imply**

`same-sector microscopic closure V x V -> V`.

The smallest decisive witness is already `n=1`, `lambda=1`:

`(2,0)*(3,0)=(6,6)`.

At `R0` the outcome is `6`; at the finite refinement `R1` the complete state `(6,6)` is revealed. The hidden component is therefore neither permanently inaccessible nor an infinite-resource artifact, yet it still exists and is dynamically generated.

## Exact proof boundary

The no-go is analytic. The refined observation is literally the identity on `A`, hence injective. Associativity follows by the Cycle-116 expansion. Generation outside `V` occurs whenever `lambda*a*b != 0`. No probabilistic or asymptotic premise is needed.

## Prior-art boundary

No novelty is claimed for algebra extensions, injective observation of an enlarged state, or the elementary fact that a finer observation can distinguish states merged by a coarser quotient. These are standard mathematical mechanisms. The PDT contribution at this stage is only the explicit no-go boundary for a proposed PDT-II derivation route.

## PDT-II consequence

A revelation principle strong enough to support same-sector closure must say more than **eventual accessibility**. It would need an independently justified prohibition or cost law saying, for example, that dynamically generated extra sectors are impossible, violate a conserved PDT resource, or produce a quantitatively excluded observable signature. Merely guaranteeing that extra sectors can later be revealed does not eliminate them.

Accordingly, finite revealability cannot be used non-circularly to obtain the closure premise needed by the known `n=3` selectors.
