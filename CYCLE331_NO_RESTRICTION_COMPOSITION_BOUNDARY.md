# PDT-II Cycle 331 — No-restriction hypothesis does not select composition or n=3

## Status

- No-restriction hypothesis (NRH): **IMPORTED/KNOWN**.
- NRH => unique PDT composition: **FALSIFIED**.
- NRH => n=3: **FALSIFIED**.
- NRH => same-input PDT/QM deviation: **FALSIFIED as an inference**.
- Dimension stress n=1..12: **PROVED for the explicit counterfamilies below**.
- PDT-native correlated-composite selector: **OPEN**.
- BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE: **NO**.

## Candidate principle attacked

A tempting closure proposal is: once the normalized state space/cone is fixed, permit every mathematically valid effect, i.e. impose the no-restriction hypothesis, and hope duality then determines the physical composite and perhaps singles out three alternatives.

This fails.

## Exact counterfamily

For every finite n >= 1 there are at least two standard finite-dimensional unrestricted theories:

1. Classical n-level theory: normalized state space is the simplex Delta_n and all affine effects 0 <= e <= u are allowed.
2. Complex quantum n-level theory: normalized states are n x n density matrices and all effects E satisfying 0 <= E <= I are allowed.

Both satisfy NRH, but for n >= 2 their state/effect geometries are inequivalent. In particular their real parameter counts are

K_C(n)=n,
K_Q(n)=n^2.

At n=2 the mismatch is already 2 versus 4, so n=2 is the smallest nontrivial decisive witness. At n=3 it is merely 3 versus 9; nothing singular occurs there.

The same counterfamily covers n=1,...,12 exactly and extends to every finite n. Thus NRH cannot select n=3.

## Composition failure

NRH is a relation between a system's state cone and its allowed effect cone. It does not supply the missing physical rule selecting a joint cone/tensor product from local systems. General GPT work distinguishes minimal/maximal/generalised tensor constructions, and theories satisfying NRH can have non-quantum composite behaviour. Therefore local dual closure cannot be promoted to a PDT-native correlated-composite law.

## Same-input prediction consequence

Complex quantum theory itself satisfies NRH. Therefore NRH alone cannot logically entail

P_PDT(O|I,R) != P_QM(O|I,R)

for identical microscopic input I and resource window R. A deviation requires an additional PDT-native operational law that excludes the quantum model and fixes quantitative probabilities.

## Prior-art audit

This is not claimed as PDT novelty. Janotta & Lal, Phys. Rev. A 87, 052131 (2013), explicitly study GPTs without the no-restriction hypothesis and note that the standard framework assumes it; they also generalize joint-state constructions. Wright & Weigert, Quantum 5, 588 (2021), characterize a broader Gleason-type class containing NRH theories. Experimental GPT work has also quantitatively bounded possible violations of NRH. Hence NRH/dual-cone closure is established prior art.

## Surviving target

The composition problem is now narrower: PDT needs an independently motivated operation on distinctions whose action on correlated objects is fixed, rather than a generic state/effect duality axiom. That operation must survive comparison against both unrestricted classical and unrestricted quantum countermodels before any n=3 or same-input deviation claim is admissible.
