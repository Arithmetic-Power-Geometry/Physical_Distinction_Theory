# Cycle 226 — First-order coherence does not determine higher-order admissibility

## Status

**PROVED (abstract countermodel)** / **FALSIFIED candidate selector** / **IMPORTED-KNOWN boundary** / **OPEN PDT-native replacement**

**BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE: NO.**

## Target attacked

PDT-II target (1): derive a PDT-native composition/admissibility law. Cycle 225 showed that local positivity or a bounded ancillary test is insufficient, while importing complete positivity is circular if Hilbert-space tensor structure is what PDT is meant to derive.

This cycle asks whether the surviving repair — specify a coherent, extension-stable first-order family of admissible states and operations — is sufficient to determine admissibility of transformations *of those operations*.

## Exact hypotheses

Let a first-order operational theory specify:

1. systems and their admissible states/effects;
2. admissible transformations between systems;
3. sequential and parallel composition of those transformations;
4. identity extensions/ancillas;
5. all first-order probabilities generated by the preceding data.

A **higher-order completion** additionally specifies which maps taking admissible transformations as inputs and returning admissible transformations are themselves physically admissible.

Candidate principle under test:

> A complete coherent first-order admissibility theory uniquely determines its higher-order admissibility/composition structure.

## Countermodel theorem

**Theorem 226.1 (first-order underdetermination of higher-order admissibility).**
There exist two operational completions with identical first-order systems, states, effects, transformations, sequential composition, parallel composition, ancilla extensions, and therefore identical first-order probabilities, but different sets of admissible higher-order transformations.

### Proof

Take any first-order theory T containing at least two distinct admissible channels and identity transformations. Construct two completions on exactly the same first-order data.

* Completion H_min admits only higher-order maps generated by ordinary fixed-order insertion into the first-order sequential/parallel circuits already present in T (plus convex mixing where T is convex).
* Completion H_ext contains every map in H_min and at least one additional well-defined higher-order transformation S on channels, while leaving every first-order object and every first-order composition rule unchanged.

By construction, restricting H_min and H_ext to first order gives exactly T. Hence every experiment containing only first-order preparations, transformations, effects, ancillas, and their ordinary compositions has identical probabilities in the two completions. But S is admissible in H_ext and not in H_min. Therefore the first-order data do not uniquely determine higher-order admissibility. QED.

The logical content is intentionally modest: this is an underdetermination theorem, not a claim that an arbitrary S is physically realizable in quantum mechanics. Its purpose is to falsify uniqueness from first-order coherence alone.

## Quantum/prior-art boundary

The physical version of this distinction is established territory. Quantum supermaps are transformations whose inputs and outputs are quantum operations; Chiribella, D'Ariano and Perinotti (EPL 83, 30004, 2008; arXiv:0804.0180) axiomatized them and proved a realization theorem for the corresponding supermaps. Bisio and Perinotti, Proc. R. Soc. A 475 (2019), arXiv:1806.09554, developed a recursive higher-order hierarchy and derive complete positivity of higher-order maps from higher-order admissibility conditions. Higher-order process theories have also been studied abstractly in process-theoretic/categorical form.

Therefore PDT cannot claim as novelty the observation that operations themselves can be resources/inputs to higher-order transformations, nor can it import the quantum-supermap hierarchy as the missing PDT law.

## Consequence for PDT-II

The Cycle-225 repair is still insufficient. A PDT-native law must be **typed/recursive across operational order**, not merely extension-stable at the state/channel level. At minimum it must answer, without importing the desired quantum structure:

    Adm_0(states/effects),
    Adm_1(transformations),
    Adm_2(transformations of transformations), ...

and state why the recursion is physically forced by distinction rather than selected by convention.

A useful surviving target is a PDT-native recursion of the form

    Adm_{k+1}(F; R) iff F preserves every admissible k-order distinction test
    under every PDT-allowed context C in a resource window R,

but this is **OPEN**: unless the class of contexts C is independently derived, the rule is circular because C already encodes the higher-order operational theory.

## n=1..12 and degenerate cases

This obstruction is structural rather than dimension-specific. It is degenerate when the first-order theory has only one admissible operation, because no nontrivial higher-order distinction is available. For every finite dimension/alphabet admitting at least two distinct first-order operations — in particular the ordinary n=2,...,12 stress range — the same minimal-vs-extended completion construction applies. Higher n does not restore uniqueness.

## Same-input prediction boundary

A difference between H_min and H_ext is **not** by itself a same-input PDT-vs-QM prediction. If PDT uses a higher-order operation unavailable in the comparator, the operational inputs differ. To satisfy the PDT-II prediction target, PDT must first derive a physically common microscopic implementation/resource window and then predict a different outcome distribution for that identical implementation.
