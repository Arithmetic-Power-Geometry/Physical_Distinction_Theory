# Cycle 229 — Information-causality selector boundary

## Target
Strongest unresolved PDT-II obligation: derive a PDT-native physical selector for composite admissibility, without importing the desired quantum composition.

## Candidate attacked
Use **information causality (IC)**, or an IC-like communication inequality, as the missing physical predicate selecting the PDT composite.

## Status
- **IMPORTED/KNOWN** as a composition-selection idea.
- **FALSIFIED** as a claim of PDT-native provenance if IC is simply adopted.
- **OPEN** whether a genuinely PDT-derived distinction principle implies IC (or a stronger complete selector) without assuming the desired composite structure.
- **BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE: NO.**

## Prior-art check
Pawłowski et al. introduced information causality as a physical principle (2009). Patra et al., *Physical Review Letters* 130, 110202 (2023), explicitly applied information causality to the **composition problem**: with local systems assumed quantum, minimal and maximal tensor-product compositions are rejected by IC, and the paper argues for an information-theoretic route toward self-duality of composite state/effect cones. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.110202; arXiv:2208.13996.

Therefore the statement

> “an information-access/communication principle physically selects composition”

cannot be claimed as PDT novelty.

## Stronger boundary
Even satisfying IC is not, from the cited result alone, a theorem that uniquely reconstructs the entire ordinary quantum composite, all admissible transformations, all higher-order maps, or a PDT-native n=3 rule. The 2023 result rejects the minimal and maximal extremes and motivates/derives self-duality under its setting; it must not be silently upgraded to uniqueness of the full quantum operational theory.

Thus the implication

\[
\mathrm{IC}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{unique full PDT/QM composite law}
\]

is **not established here** and remains **OPEN** unless separately proved under explicit hypotheses.

## Non-circularity consequence
Adopting IC as an extra axiom may constrain PDT composites, but it does not meet the current PDT-II requirement of a **PDT-native** selector. The only admissible route is to derive an information-access bound from prior PDT primitives (distinction, resource window, refinement/revelation accounting, etc.) and then prove what composite structures it excludes/selects.

If such a derivation merely reproduces known IC, classify the result **IMPORTED/KNOWN consequence**, not breakthrough. A breakthrough candidate would require a PDT-native stronger theorem or a new falsifiable inequality not already contained in IC/GPT/resource-theory results.

## n=3 consequence
IC does not by itself single out the integer 3. Consequently it cannot be used as the requested non-circular PDT-native derivation of n=3 without an additional theorem connecting PDT primitives to a dimension-dependent bound with a unique optimizer at n=3.

## Same-input prediction consequence
A PDT–QM probability difference obtained only by imposing IC in PDT but not imposing the same operational restriction in the QM comparator is not a valid same-input prediction. The microscopic preparation, allowed communication/couplings, measurement, and resource window must be held fixed.

## Next strongest attack
Derive, rather than posit, a **PDT distinction-access inequality** from the existing refinement/revelation ledger. Required proof obligations:
1. exact hypotheses and resource accounting;
2. validity under composition and ancillary extension;
3. n=1..12 exact/numerical stress tests where a concrete finite model is available;
4. adversarial search against classical, quantum, GPT and restricted-resource models;
5. determine whether the inequality is merely IC/data processing/Holevo-type prior art;
6. only then test whether it excludes multiple coherent composite completions.

No gravity/capacity law is promoted in this cycle.
