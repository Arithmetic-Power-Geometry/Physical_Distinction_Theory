# Cycle 262 — Restricted distinction norms do not select composition

## Targets attacked
1. PDT-native composition law.
3. Same-input quantitative PDT-vs-QM prediction.
4. Resource refinement/revelation.
5. Experimentally distinctive inequalities.

## Candidate principle
Try to select the composite by requiring a PDT distinction functional under a restricted resource window R to factor/multiply on product differences, or by requiring a fixed local-to-global distinguishability ratio.

For a measurement family M_R define the operational distinguishability seminorm

    ||x||_{M_R} = sup_{(e_i) in M_R} sum_i |e_i(x)|.

The hope is that local norms plus a prescribed product rule determine the joint cone/effect set.

## Result: norm-data composition underdetermination
This route fails as a unique selector.

### Proposition
Local distinguishability norms and their values on product tensors do not, in general, determine the admissible composite cone or the full joint measurement family.

### Proof/obstruction
A norm records only the support function generated by the allowed effects on state differences. Distinct joint effect sets can have the same balanced convex hull and therefore induce the same distinguishability norm. Even fixing the norm on every simple tensor x_A tensor x_B constrains only a thin subset of the joint vector space; it does not determine its values on general sums of tensors, nor which positive tensors are states. Consequently it cannot select a unique point between admissible min/max composite constructions without an additional cross-system axiom.

A particularly sharp resource-window version follows immediately: two resource families whose effect sets have the same closed balanced convex hull are operationally indistinguishable for every binary discrimination task although their primitive measurement implementations may differ. Hence binary distinction data alone cannot reconstruct composition.

## Same-input consequence
If PDT defines its operational predictions only through the same restricted measurement family M_R and the same states as QM, then equality of the induced support functional implies equality of every binary discrimination bias. A PDT-vs-QM probability deviation therefore cannot be inferred merely from a new name for the norm or from factorization on product inputs. It requires a genuinely different state/effect/dynamics/admissibility primitive and an experiment probing that difference.

## Dimension stress
The obstruction is linear/convex-geometric rather than dimension-specific. It already appears whenever the joint tensor space contains non-simple tensors; embedding a fixed witness into larger spaces preserves it. Therefore n=1 is degenerate, while n>=2 does not single out n=3. Exact dimension enumeration cannot repair the missing cross-system information.

## Prior-art audit
Restricted-measurement distinguishability norms and quantum data hiding are established prior art (Matthews-Wehner-Winter 2009; Aubrun-Lancien; Lami-Palazuelos-Winter). The GPT data-hiding literature explicitly connects distinguishability ratios to projective/injective tensor-norm geometry. Local discriminability itself is also an established operational-probabilistic axiom and does not uniquely fix composition. Therefore neither the norm construction nor generic data-hiding inequalities are PDT-native novelty.

## Surviving PDT requirement
A viable PDT composition principle must constrain genuinely joint distinctions beyond the balanced support data of binary discrimination and beyond product-factorization. It must specify what distinguishes two candidate composites that agree on all local/product distinction tests. Candidate primitives must then be checked for associativity, positivity, reversible-group compatibility, classical collapse, quantum/Jordan/GPT countermodels, and resource quotients.
