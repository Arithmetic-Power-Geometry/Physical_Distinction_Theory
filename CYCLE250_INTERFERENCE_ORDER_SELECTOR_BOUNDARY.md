# Cycle 250 — Interference-order selector boundary

## Target
Test whether a PDT-native candidate of the form “distinction interference terminates at order 2” can (i) determine the composite law or (ii) non-circularly select n=3.

## Exact hypotheses tested
Let a theory admit n-way alternatives and Sorkin-type interference functionals I_k. Candidate C250 asserts I_k=0 for every k>=3, with ordinary normalization/positivity. No dimension-dependent constant and no occurrence of the desired value n=3 is permitted in the axiom.

## Result 250A — dimension-selection no-go (PROVED)
If C250 is imposed uniformly on all finite systems, then it cannot select n=3. Complex quantum theory supplies a counterfamily in every finite Hilbert dimension n: Born probabilities have no irreducible third- or higher-order Sorkin interference, while systems exist for n=1,2,3,... . In particular n=2 is the smallest nontrivial competitor to n=3.

This is an analytic counterfamily, not a numerical inference. The n=1..12 stress window is therefore automatically covered.

## Result 250B — composition-selection no-go (PROVED)
Absence of third/higher-order interference constrains an operational probability calculus but does not uniquely determine a tensor/composite rule. The property is already shared by inequivalent operational theories (at minimum classical probability and finite-dimensional complex quantum theory), whose state spaces and composites are not the same. Hence C250 cannot by itself be J_PDT.

## Adversarial boundary
The opposite move — postulating genuine third-order interference because “PDT wants three” — is circular as an n=3 derivation and is experimentally non-QM rather than a derivation of QM. Density-cube models are explicit prior-art examples of third-order interference, so the mere existence of an I_3 term is not PDT novelty.

## Prior-art boundary
- Sorkin's interference hierarchy predates PDT; classical theory is first-order and quantum theory second-order.
- Barnum, Lee, Scandolo and Selby (2017), arXiv:1704.05106, derive absence of higher-order interference from established GPT purity principles; they explicitly note this does not recover the whole quantum formalism.
- Barnum and Hilgert (2019), arXiv:1904.03753, show strong symmetry + spectrality already lead to Jordan-algebraic state spaces, making no-higher-order-interference superfluous in that route.
- Dakic, Paterek and Brukner (2013), arXiv:1308.2822, construct density-cube theories with genuine third-order interference and non-quantum predictions.

Therefore relabelling Sorkin order as “distinction order” is IMPORTED/KNOWN unless a new PDT primitive independently derives a quantitatively different functional.

## Same-input prediction audit
No same-input P_PDT(O|I,R) != P_QM(O|I,R) follows from C250. Setting I_3=0 reproduces a known quantum-compatible constraint; setting I_3!=0 requires a new magnitude/sign/dynamics law and calibrated resource window, none presently derived.

## Status
- No-higher-order interference as n=3 selector: **FALSIFIED**.
- No-higher-order interference as unique composition selector: **FALSIFIED**.
- Dimension-uniform counterfamily: **PROVED**.
- Sorkin/GPT interference hierarchy: **IMPORTED/KNOWN**.
- PDT-native quantitative I_3 law: **OPEN**.
- Same-input PDT-vs-QM deviation: **OPEN**.
- BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE: **NO**.

## Surviving obligation
A viable PDT composition principle must determine admissible joint structure from PDT primitives rather than from a dimension-blind interference-order cutoff. A viable n=3 derivation must contain an independently derived feasibility obstruction that fails for n=2 and n>=4 without encoding 3 in its definitions or constants.
