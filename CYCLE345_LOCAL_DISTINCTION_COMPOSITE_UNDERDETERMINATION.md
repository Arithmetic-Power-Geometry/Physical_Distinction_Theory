# Cycle 345 — Local-distinction composite underdetermination

## Target
Attack PDT-II target (1) at its strongest surviving boundary: can complete single-system distinction data determine a unique correlated composite?

## Candidate principle
Let a local operational system A be specified by its normalized state space, effect space, admissible reversible transformations, and therefore every single-system distinction statistic obtainable from those data. Candidate claim: this complete local distinction structure uniquely determines the admissible bipartite composite A⊗A.

## Prove-or-falsify result
The claim is false in general probabilistic theories.

For ordered vector spaces A and B, once the local positive cones A_+ and B_+ are fixed, there are in general at least two canonical composite cones consistent with the same local systems:

1. the minimal tensor cone, generated by positive product tensors a⊗b;
2. the maximal tensor cone, consisting of tensors positive on every product effect.

They have exactly the same local state/effect data by construction, while for non-simplicial systems they can differ strictly. The minimal composite contains only convex mixtures of product states; the maximal composite can contain additional correlated states. Hence even *complete local distinction data*, not merely one scalar metric, cannot by itself select the correlated composite.

This is stronger than Cycles 343–344: failure is not caused by choosing TV rather than a tensorizing metric. The missing information is genuinely compositional.

## Small decisive witness
A non-simplicial binary-input/binary-output square state space (the standard gbit/boxworld local geometry) admits distinct minimal and maximal tensor products. Both composites retain the same local square, the same local effects and the same local distinguishability relations, but their bipartite state sets differ: the minimal tensor contains separable mixtures of product states, whereas the maximal tensor admits nonclassical no-signalling correlations.

Thus a local PDT distinction object D_A, even if it contains *all* local pairwise distinguishabilities, cannot reconstruct the composite unless PDT supplies an additional genuinely compositional axiom.

## Dimension stress
The obstruction is structural rather than a dimension-specific numerical accident.

- n=1: trivial system; no nontrivial composite ambiguity.
- n=2 onward: any admissible PDT family that contains a non-simplicial local subsystem inherits the min/max ambiguity unless an additional composition axiom removes it.
- n=2,...,12: no logical mechanism in local distinction data singles out n=3; embedding a fixed non-simplicial witness with spectator/trivial sectors preserves the underdetermination.
- higher finite dimensions: the same ordered-vector-space min/max construction applies whenever the local cone is not in a class for which the two tensor cones collapse.
- classical simplices are an important edge case: their standard tensor structure does not furnish this witness; they therefore do not rescue the universal uniqueness claim.

This cycle does not pretend that the spectator embedding proves every conceivable meaning of PDT dimension n. It proves the sharper universal statement: local operational/distinction structure alone is insufficient in the presence of a non-simplicial admissible local model.

## Same-input prediction consequence
If two theories share identical local inputs I and local resource window R but choose different admissible composite cones, their joint predictions can differ. However, this observation does NOT produce a PDT prediction: PDT still needs a native rule selecting one composite and a measurement/probability rule. Therefore no parameter-free P_PDT(O|I,R) != P_QM(O|I,R) is claimed.

## Prior-art audit
The min/max tensor-product ambiguity is established GPT machinery, not PDT novelty. Recent GPT work explicitly states that composite systems are not uniquely defined and that state spaces can range between minimal and maximal tensor products. Patra et al. (arXiv:2208.13996; later PRL) likewise discuss minimal and maximal compositions even when local systems are fixed and use information causality as an additional selector. Therefore the underdetermination theorem is IMPORTED/KNOWN as GPT structure; its value here is as a decisive no-go boundary for PDT-II.

## Strengthened surviving target
Do not search for another local scalar or even another complete local metric as the composition selector. The next viable candidate must constrain joint states directly. A PDT-native candidate must specify, without importing the desired quantum tensor product, a joint distinction consistency condition C_AB whose allowed cone is uniquely determined, then survive min/max-GPT adversaries, classical/simplex edge cases, quantum composites, restricted-resource quotients, and n=1,...,12 stress.
