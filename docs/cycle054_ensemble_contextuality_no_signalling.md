# Cycle 054 — Ensemble-contextual nonlinear same-input escape is incompatible with remote-steering no-signalling

## Target
PDT-II target (3): seek a quantitative same-input prediction

\[
P_{\rm PDT}(O\mid I,R)\ne P_{\rm QM}(O\mid I,R)
\]

without silently changing the microscopic input, measurement effect, or declared resource window.

## Candidate attacked
A tempting escape from the affine/Born locks is to let the outcome probability depend nonlinearly on the pure-state components of a preparation ensemble, rather than only on the density operator. Schematically, for an ensemble \(\{w_k,|\psi_k\rangle\}\),

\[
P(E)=\sum_k w_k\,g(\langle\psi_k|E|\psi_k\rangle),
\]

with nonlinear \(g\).

## Minimal exact witness
Take Bob's local density operator

\[
\rho_B=I/2.
\]

It has two equal-weight decompositions:

* Z decomposition: \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\);
* X decomposition: \(|+\rangle,|-\rangle\).

For Bob's effect \(E=|0\rangle\langle0|\), the component Born overlaps are

\[
Z:(1,0),\qquad X:(1/2,1/2).
\]

Choose the nonlinear response \(g(x)=x^2\). Then

\[
P_Z=\tfrac12(1^2+0^2)=\tfrac12,
\]

while

\[
P_X=\tfrac12[(1/2)^2+(1/2)^2]=\tfrac14.
\]

The local density operator and local measurement are identical, yet the predicted local statistics depend on which decomposition is realized.

By the Hughston-Jozsa-Wootters remote-ensemble theorem, alternative ensemble decompositions of the same reduced state can be generated by suitable measurements on a purifying remote system. Therefore, if the remote party can freely select between these decompositions while Bob's local input and measurement remain fixed, a decomposition-sensitive response produces a signalling channel.

## Surviving theorem
Under the assumptions:

1. standard quantum kinematics and purification/remote steering are retained;
2. remote choices may realize different ensemble decompositions of the same reduced density operator;
3. Bob's local outcome law depends operationally on that decomposition;
4. spacelike no-signalling is required;

Bob's observable probabilities must be invariant under all remotely realizable decompositions of the same reduced state. Hence an ensemble-contextual nonlinear rule cannot serve as a same-local-input PDT deviation unless at least one of the assumptions above is explicitly changed.

This does **not** prove that every conceivable nonlinear or nonstandard theory signals. It kills the specific escape route in which remotely selectable decompositions of one density operator are assigned different local statistics while standard steering and no-signalling are retained.

## Prior-art boundary
The underlying obstruction is not historically novel. It is in the Gisin/Polchinski/no-signalling lineage, and HJW supplies the remote-ensemble realization. Simon, Buzek and Gisin (2001) further showed strong linearity constraints from no-signalling under standard quantum-state/probability assumptions. PDT's contribution here is a precise kill-test for target (3), not a claim of discovering the no-signalling theorem.

## Classification

- **FALSIFIED:** ensemble-decomposition-sensitive nonlinear response as a free same-input PDT escape while retaining standard remote steering and no-signalling.
- **PROVED:** exact qubit witness for \(g(x)=x^2\).
- **IMPORTED/KNOWN:** HJW remote ensemble theorem and Gisin/Polchinski/no-signalling obstruction.
- **OPEN:** a PDT-native quantitative deviation that changes an explicit physical law while remaining compatible with experimentally required causal constraints.

## Consequence for PDT-II
The target-(3) search space is narrower. A viable PDT prediction must expose its changed physical coordinate explicitly: modified dynamics/process, modified admissible physical effect, nonstandard global state/composition rule, restricted steering structure, or another independently testable law. Merely making probabilities nonlinear in an unobservable preparation decomposition is not acceptable.
