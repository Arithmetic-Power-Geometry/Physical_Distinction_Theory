## I. State-resolution closure

### SR1 — Elementary binary rank
Every irreducible elementary system has maximal perfectly distinguishable frame size two.

### SR2 — Erasure neutrality
For every maximal perfectly distinguishable pair `(alpha,beta)`, unbiased complete erasure yields the unique reversible-invariant state `mu`:

`(alpha+beta)/2 = mu`.

### SR3 — No hidden residual distinction
Every elementary state is completely specified by one maximal binary distinction and its bias:

`omega = p alpha + (1-p) beta`, `p in [0,1]`.

### SR4 — Continuous reversible pure transitivity
The connected reversible group acts transitively on pure states.

### Theorem SR
Under SR1–SR4, the centered elementary state space is an ellipsoid and is affinely equivalent to a Euclidean ball `B^n`.

#### Proof
Let `K = Omega-mu` and `x = alpha-mu`. From SR2, `beta-mu=-x`. From SR3,

`omega-mu=(2p-1)x`.

Hence `K` is centrally symmetric and every state lies on a radial segment joining antipodal pure points. Interior radial points have coefficient magnitude strictly less than one, hence are interior; therefore the entire boundary is pure. SR4 makes the reversible group transitive on the full boundary. Since `mu` is fixed, reversible affine maps become linear on the centered space and preserve `K`. Average any auxiliary positive-definite inner product over the compact reversible group. The resulting inner product is invariant. Since the group is transitive on the entire boundary, the invariant Euclidean norm is constant on the boundary. Therefore the boundary is an ellipsoid; after affine normalization, `K=B^n`. QED.

### Corollary SR-BQDC
Define `Q_D(x)=<x,x>_D`. Then

`Q_D(x+y)+Q_D(x-y)=2Q_D(x)+2Q_D(y)`.

Thus BQDC is derived from SR1–SR4.

## III. Absolute horizon normalization closure

A scale-free distinction ordering cannot fix an absolute coefficient. Therefore an absolute calibration principle is mathematically necessary.

### AC1 — Distinction entropy calibration
One resolved binary distinction carries thermodynamic entropy

`Delta S = k_B ln 2`

This is the standard bit-entropy calibration and is an imported thermodynamic normalization, not derivable from a scale-free code ordering alone.

### AC2 — Relativistic localization bound
For a system of total energy `E` confined to radius `R`, accessible distinction entropy obeys the Bekenstein form

`S <= 2 pi k_B E R/(hbar c)`.

In bits,

`K <= 2 pi E R/(hbar c ln 2)`.

### AC3 — Schwarzschild saturation
For a stationary Schwarzschild horizon, take `E=Mc^2` and `R=2GM/c^2`, and assume the horizon saturates the accessible-distinction bound.

Then

`K_H = 2 pi (Mc^2)(2GM/c^2)/(hbar c ln2)`

`= 4 pi G M^2/(hbar c ln2)`.

Since

`A = 4 pi R^2 = 16 pi G^2 M^2/c^4`

and

`l_P^2 = G hbar/c^3`,

we have

`A/(4 l_P^2 ln2) = 4 pi G M^2/(hbar c ln2)`.

Therefore

`K_H = A/(4 l_P^2 ln2)`.

This fixes the exact coefficient conditionally on AC1–AC3. The coefficient is not a PDT-only prediction unless AC1–AC3 are themselves derived from deeper PDT dynamics.
